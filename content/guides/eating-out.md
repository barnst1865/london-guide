London restaurants run on a slightly different rhythm from the ones at home, and the bill works differently too. None of it is hard, but a few things will save you a cold walk to a closed door or an awkward moment at the end of dinner. For the places themselves, start with [Where We Eat](theme:eat).

## When you need to book (and when you can't)

Book dinner on a Friday or Saturday. Those are the nights when a good restaurant fills up, and "we'll just see if they have a table" is a gamble you don't want to take with hungry kids.

A few places don't take reservations at all. The restaurant's own website will say so, and it's worth checking before you set out. For the rest, booking on the restaurant's own site is usually easy.

## Service charge and tipping

Look at the bill before you tip. Many London restaurants add a service charge, commonly 12.5%, and it's usually listed as a line item. If it's on the bill, you don't need to leave anything extra. [Visit London's guide to tipping](https://visitlondon.com/traveller-information/essential-information/money/tipping) notes that a service charge is discretionary, even if added, and that you can ask for it to be removed if you're unhappy with the service. If there isn't one, it's customary to leave a modest tip.

Tipping isn't expected in pubs, or when you're picking up takeout. Visit London's guide covers taxis, hotels and the rest if you want the full picture.

One more thing that surprises Americans: the bill usually doesn't arrive until you ask for it. Nobody is trying to hurry you out. When you're ready, catch an eye and ask for "the bill."

## Sunday roasts

A Sunday roast is a roast meat with potatoes, vegetables, gravy and a Yorkshire pudding (see the [glossary](guide:britishisms)), and it's a proper event. Book ahead, because the popular places sell out, and the best ones can run out of beef by mid-afternoon.

It's a lunch, not a dinner, so plan to eat at midday or early afternoon rather than hoping for a roast at 7 p.m. Most places that do roasts also have vegetarian options. Sunday lunch with kids tends to go well, and a pub is often the easiest place to do it; there's more on that in [pub etiquette](guide:pub-etiquette).

## Eating early, eating late

Kitchens close earlier than you'd think. The lights can be on and the staff still around long after the kitchen has stopped taking orders, so don't assume a lit window means you can still eat. If you're hungry late, check the restaurant's hours online, or plan to eat somewhere that is set up for late service.

Check Sundays too. Some places close on Sunday evening, and a few don't open at all, so look at the restaurant's own website before you head out.
