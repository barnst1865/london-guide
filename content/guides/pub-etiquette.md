A London pub is the easiest place in the city to get small things wrong and the easiest to enjoy once you know the rhythm. Nobody will be rude to you about it, but a few habits will make the whole thing smoother. For the words you'll hear at the bar, see the [glossary of Britishisms](guide:britishisms).

## Order at the bar (and no table service)

Pubs mostly don't have table service. Find a seat first if you can, then go to the bar to order and pay. You pay when you order, not at the end, and the drinks are handed over right away. Some pubs ask for a table number when you order food, so look for one on your table or on the menu.

Don't wave, snap your fingers or shout. Bar staff serve people in the order they arrived and have a good sense of who's next, so catch an eye, wait your turn and be patient on a busy night.

There's no tipping in a pub. [Visit London's tipping guide](https://visitlondon.com/traveller-information/essential-information/money/tipping) says people generally don't tip in bars and pubs, though a tray or jar may appear when your change comes back. Ignoring it is fine.

## Rounds

In a small group of friends, drinks usually come in rounds: one person buys for everyone, the next person gets the next round, and everyone takes a turn. If you're in a small group, expect to buy one. In a bigger group, or with people you've just met, buy your own and nobody will think twice.

You can order a pint or a half pint. A pint is about 568 milliliters, a bit bigger than the US pint, so a half is plenty if you're pacing yourself.

## Kids and dogs in pubs

Plenty of pubs welcome families, especially ones that serve food, and kids are generally fine until early evening. After that, many pubs are just pubs. Dogs are usually welcome, but ask at the door. Sunday lunch with kids tends to work well, and we have more on that in [eating out](guide:eating-out).

Each pub sets its own policy, and the law also has a say. [GOV.UK explains the rules](https://www.gov.uk/alcohol-young-people-law) for children under 16 and for 16- and 17-year-olds, including the exception that lets a 16- or 17-year-old have a beer, wine or cider with a meal when accompanied by an adult. If it matters, check the pub's own website.

## Last orders and closing time

"Last orders" is your final chance to order at the bar, and a bell often rings to tell you. Closing times vary from pub to pub, so check the pub's own website or its listing if you're planning around it. Kitchens often stop serving food well before the bar stops serving drinks.

## Samuel Smith's pubs: a special case

[Samuel Smith's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Smith_Old_Brewery) is a Yorkshire brewery that owns a number of old pubs in central London with historic interiors, and it runs them in its own way. Every pub sells only the brewery's own brand of drinks, so don't expect the usual lagers. And there's a rule worth knowing before you walk in: no phones, tablets or laptops indoors. The aim is to encourage conversation. The brewery is also reputed to be strict about swearing.

Ye Olde Cheshire Cheese, off Fleet Street, is a Samuel Smith's pub, so it's a good place to see all of this in action. Put your phone in your pocket before you go through the door.
