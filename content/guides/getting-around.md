London's transport is easy once you've got three habits: tap a card, use one good app, and walk more than you think you need to. Fares, caps and service changes are TfL's business (Transport for London runs the Tube, buses and most of the rest), so we link to their pages rather than quote numbers that will be out of date by the time you read this.

**Traveling with kids?** Children under 11 travel free on most London transport. Children aged 11 to 15 can get half-price fares with the Young Visitor discount, but only on a Visitor Oyster card, not on contactless: once you've arrived, take their card to staff at most Tube stations or a TfL Visitor Centre and ask for it to be added. It lasts 14 days. And everyone aged 11 or over needs their own card or phone to tap: you can't pay for two people with one card. [TfL's comparison](https://support.visitorshop.tfl.gov.uk/hc/en-gb/articles/28090497795346-Visitor-Oyster-card-vs-Contactless-which-is-best-for-your-London-trip) has the details.

## Paying: tap in, tap out

Skip the ticket machines. Tap a contactless card or a phone on the yellow reader as you go through the gate, and tap again on the way out. That's the whole system. Fares are capped automatically, so if you ride a lot in one day you stop paying at some point; [TfL explains how capping works](https://tfl.gov.uk/fares/find-fares/capping) and keeps the current numbers.

Buses and trams are the exception: tap in when you get on, and don't tap out. [TfL's pay-as-you-go page](https://tfl.gov.uk/fares/how-to-pay-and-where-to-buy-tickets-and-oyster/pay-as-you-go/contactless-and-mobile-pay-as-you-go) has the details, including which cards and devices work.

Three things we'd tell any friend:

- **Use the same card or device every time.** TfL says to touch in and out with the same one, and it matters. Tap in with your phone and out with your card and you risk paying the wrong fare. Capping is also tracked per card or device, so splitting your trips between two means neither gets the full benefit.
- **If you have an iPhone, turn on Express Transit.** It lets your phone pay at the gate without waking up and waiting for Face ID, which keeps you from holding up the line behind you. [Apple's instructions](https://support.apple.com/en-us/105123) show where the setting lives in the Wallet app.
- **Check how your card handles foreign fees.** TfL notes that a mobile payment linked to a non-UK bank card may not work, or may carry overseas transaction fees. Ask your bank before you fly.

For adults, we don't bother with an Oyster card or a Visitor Oyster on a normal trip, because contactless does the same job without a deposit or a trip to a shop. The exception is kids aged 11 to 15 (see above). If you want to run the numbers for a long stay, [TfL has its own comparison](https://visitorshop.tfl.gov.uk/en/visitor-oyster-card-vs-contactless-bank-card-which-saves-you-more-money-london).

## The apps we use

[Citymapper](https://citymapper.com/) is the one we use. It compares the Tube, buses, trains and walking for the same trip and shows how long each will take, which is usually all the planning you need. For the official word on delays, closures and weekend engineering works, [TfL's live status page](https://tfl.gov.uk/tube-dlr-overground/status/) is the source, and [TfL Go](https://tfl.gov.uk/maps_/tfl-go) is their own app.

## Tube, bus, Overground, Elizabeth line: which when

The Tube is the fastest way to cover distance. The Overground, the Elizabeth line and the DLR all work the same way (tap in, tap out) and appear on the same Tube map, so you can mix them freely. On escalators, stand on the right and leave the left side clear for people in a hurry.

Buses are slower, and when you're sightseeing that's the point. Take a regular route, climb to the top deck, grab the front seat and you get a tour of the city for the price of a fare, with no ticket to buy. Contactless works on buses, tap in only. Citymapper will tell you which route.

## Walking is often faster

The Tube map is a diagram, not a map. It straightens the lines and spaces the stations out evenly, which makes the center look bigger than it is. Many stations in the middle of town are a few minutes apart on foot, and with the stairs, the escalators and the wait for a train, walking can win.

TfL's own blog has a list: [Monument to London Bridge is about a five-minute walk](https://londonblog.tfl.gov.uk/2020/07/16/station-walks/), and Waterloo to Southwark is about four. If you're not sure, open the map on your phone and look at the distance before you dive underground. TfL also publishes [a walking Tube map](https://tfl.gov.uk/cdn/static/cms/documents/walking-tube-map-zones-1-3.pdf) for the central zones.

One safety note: traffic drives on the left, so look right first when you step off the curb. Many crossings have LOOK RIGHT painted on the pavement as a reminder, and it's worth heeding, especially when you've just landed.

## Getting to and from the airports

**Heathrow:** the Elizabeth line is our default. It runs straight into central London, you can pay with the same contactless card you use everywhere else, and [TfL's Heathrow page](https://tfl.gov.uk/modes/elizabeth-line/getting-to-and-from-heathrow-on-the-elizabeth-line) explains the stops and any special fares. The [Heathrow Express](https://www.heathrowexpress.com/) is a non-stop train to Paddington and is worth considering when Paddington is where you're headed.

**Gatwick, Stansted, Luton and London City:** these are further out or more complicated, and the best route depends on where you're staying. Start with each airport's own "getting here" page and plug your hotel into Citymapper.

For anything else, [TfL's journey planner](https://tfl.gov.uk/plan-a-journey/) is the official answer.
