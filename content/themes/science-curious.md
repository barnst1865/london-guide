London has been a city of inventors, engineers and doctors for centuries, and plenty of the evidence is still lying around.

Start in Soho at [John Snow's pump](place:broad-street-pump), where tracing an 1854 cholera outbreak to the water helped invent epidemiology. Engineering fans should head to Rotherhithe for the [Brunel Museum](place:brunel-museum), at the start of the first tunnel under a navigable river, or to Greenwich, where you can walk beneath the hull of the [Cutty Sark](place:cutty-sark). Out on the Greenwich Peninsula, the [cable gear at Enderby Wharf](place:enderby-wharf-cable-gear) marks where the Great Eastern's telegraph cables were made.

[Alexandra Palace](place:alexandra-palace) is where the BBC began regular television in 1936, and the view is worth the climb. Smaller and odder: the [London Canal Museum](place:london-canal-museum) is in an old ice warehouse with an ice well you can peer into, and the [Crystal Palace Museum](place:crystal-palace-museum) keeps the memory of Paxton's glass palace alive in a single room.
