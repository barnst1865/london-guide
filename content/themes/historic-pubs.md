If you only do one thing from this guide, make it an afternoon in old pubs. London still has hundreds of them, and the best are living history you can sit down in: rooms rebuilt after the Great Fire, Victorian bars divided into snugs, and riverside taverns that served sailors long before they served tourists.

Every pub and restaurant here is at least 75 years old, and most are much older. A few rules of thumb:

- **Old stories need a pinch of salt.** Nearly every old pub claims a famous regular, a secret tunnel, or a date carved over the door. We've checked the claims we could. Where something is only legend, we say so, and the true stories are usually better.
- **The oldest-looking pubs aren't always the oldest.** Some of London's most "medieval" interiors were built in the 1920s. That doesn't make them less fun.
- **The City shuts at weekends.** Many of the best old pubs in the Square Mile open only Monday to Friday, so plan your City crawl for a weekday afternoon.
- **Samuel Smith's pubs have house rules.** Cheap beer, no phones, no swearing. Take your calls outside.

New to British pubs? You order and pay at the bar, and nobody comes to take your order.

Start with [Ye Olde Cheshire Cheese](place:ye-olde-cheshire-cheese), the one we take everyone to first, then follow the [City of London pub crawl](trail:city-pub-crawl) east from Hatton Garden to Bank.
