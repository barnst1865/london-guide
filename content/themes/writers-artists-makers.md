London has housed more writers and artists than we could ever list, and a handful of their homes and workshops are open to visit.

In Bloomsbury, the [Charles Dickens Museum](place:charles-dickens-museum) is his only surviving London home, where he wrote Oliver Twist. A short walk away, the [Foundling Museum](place:foundling-museum) tells the moving story of the Foundling Hospital, with Hogarth's paintings and Handel's music. William Morris has two stops: the free [gallery](place:william-morris-gallery) in his family's old house in Walthamstow, and the [William Morris Society](place:william-morris-society) at Kelmscott House in Hammersmith, a few doors from [the Dove](place:the-dove-hammersmith).

For fiction come to life, the [Sherlock Holmes Museum](place:sherlock-holmes-museum) on Baker Street sets out 221B as Holmes and Watson's rooms; book ahead. And on South Molton Street, a plaque marks the [building](place:william-blake-south-molton-street) where William Blake lived and made Milton and Jerusalem.
