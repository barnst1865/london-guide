London is the spy capital of the world, and almost none of it is behind glass. The Cambridge Five drank in these pubs and shared flats on these streets; MI5 and MI6 ran the early Cold War from anonymous office blocks in Mayfair and Westminster that you can walk past today without a flicker; the KGB left messages in lamp posts and church porticos. And in this century two Russian defectors were killed here with weapons from a Bond villain's cupboard, polonium in a teapot and a nerve agent on a door handle, which is why we get serious as well as curious.

This theme is mostly a walking theme. Our [WMD Tour](trail:wmd-tour) strings the darkest of it together in a day, from a Soho water pump that founded epidemiology to the bridge where Georgi Markov was jabbed with a ricin pellet, with the government's nuclear bunker and Churchill's war rooms in between. Around it are the places we'd add on a second day: the hotel where MI6 recruited Philby over tea, the Mayfair square where a lamp post is said to have been a dead-letter box, the square where Anthony Blunt confessed, the river view of the two intelligence headquarters that have played themselves in every Bond film since the nineties.

A few ground rules we set ourselves. Where history happened at someone's home, we give you the street or the square and never the door. Where a building is still a working government site, we tell you only what the government itself has said about it. And where a story is good but unproven, from the umbrella to the chalk marks, we say so: "said to be" is doing honest work on these pages. The real people in these stories, Alexander Litvinenko, Georgi Markov, Dawn Sturgess, deserve that.

Day trips to Bletchley Park, the Kelvedon Hatch bunker and Salisbury are coming once the London pages have settled.
