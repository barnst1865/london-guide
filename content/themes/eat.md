London is one of the great places in the world to eat, and also one of the easiest places to eat badly: the streets around the big sights are lined with chains and tourist menus. These are the places we actually go to, and the ones we send friends to.

We lean toward places that have been doing one thing well for a long time, at a fair price, and that would be here whether or not visitors came. That means family-run dining rooms like [Punjab](place:punjab), which has been on Neal Street since 1951; East End institutions like [E Pellicci](place:e-pellicci) and [Tayyabs](place:tayyabs); and small, honest kitchens in the middle of town like [Tokyo Diner](place:tokyo-diner). A few are splurges, a few are here for the setting (a café over a canal tunnel, a terrace on the Thames), and a couple are chains with a good reason to be included.

Some practical notes. London's food is spread out, and some of the best of it is in neighborhoods visitors never reach, so it's worth crossing town for Whitechapel's grill houses. Plenty of the cheaper places are bring-your-own, so check before you go and pick up a bottle on the way. Book anywhere popular for a Friday or Saturday night, and don't be surprised by a service charge already on the bill. Our [eating-out guide](guide:eating-out) explains booking, tipping and Sunday roasts.

For pubs and restaurants with a long history, see [Historic Pubs & Restaurants](theme:historic-pubs). Several of the oldest places here appear in both.
