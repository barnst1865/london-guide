The Blitz hit the City of London hardest on the night of 29 December 1940, when incendiaries set the whole square mile around St Paul's alight and eight of Wren's churches burned; the cathedral survived because three hundred volunteers of the St Paul's Watch spent the night on its roofs putting out fires. The raids of April and May 1941 finished the job. The City rebuilt itself as offices, but it made a series of quiet, rather moving decisions about the churches it had lost: keep this tower, plant this nave as a garden, give that one to the Americans.

This walk links those decisions. It is flat, mostly free, and threads through back lanes that the office crowds use and tourists do not, so weekends are empty and weekday lunchtimes are lively. Children do better on it than you would expect, because each stop is a small puzzle: why is there a tower in the road, why are there roses where the pews were, why is there a church missing. The whole thing is about two miles.

The Smithfield V-2 memorial is ten minutes north of the start if you want to add the rocket war to the bombing war, and the V&A's shrapnel wall in South Kensington is the same idea on the other side of town.
