If the [Battle of Britain Bunker](place:battle-of-britain-bunker) at Uxbridge is where the squadrons were sent up, Bentley Priory is where the decisions came from. Fighter Command made its headquarters in this Georgian mansion on the northern edge of London in 1936, and Air Chief Marshal Sir Hugh Dowding ran the Battle of Britain from an office upstairs that the museum has kept with his furniture in place. Downstairs was the Filter Room, where the raw plots from the coastal radar chain were sifted and checked before being passed on to the group operations rooms; the whole "Dowding system" that won the battle starts in this house.

The museum opened in 2013, after the RAF left and the grounds were sold for housing, and it is a quieter, more museum-like experience than Uxbridge: Dowding's office, the Battle of Britain lace panel, Luftwaffe trophies including a bust of Göring, films, and gardens to run around in. It suits children from about eight.

It is open only three days a week (Wednesday, Friday, Saturday, plus bank-holiday Mondays) and closed for a month over Christmas, so check before you set out. Getting there is the catch: Stanmore on the Jubilee line, then a bus or a long walk.

**Our tip:** Do Uxbridge in the morning and Bentley Priory in the afternoon only if you have a car; otherwise give it its own half-day and walk up from Stanmore through the common.
