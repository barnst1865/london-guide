William Morris, the designer, poet and socialist, lived at Kelmscott House from 1878 until he died in 1896, and named it after his country house in Oxfordshire. The Georgian house itself (around 1785, Grade II* listed) is private, but the William Morris Society runs a small museum in the coach house and lower rooms, with a garden behind.

The displays cover Morris at home in Hammersmith: his wallpapers and textiles, his printing, and the socialist meetings he held in the coach house. His Kelmscott Press worked a few doors along Upper Mall from 1891. Plaques on the house remember two other residents: the novelist George MacDonald, and Francis Ronalds, who ran an experimental electric telegraph through the garden in 1816. Kids get a free activity trail. Allow about half an hour.

**Our tip:** It's only open a few days a week, so check the website before you build the walk around it.
