Hidden in the maze of alleys behind Cornhill, the Jamaica Wine House, known to regulars as "the Jampot," is our favorite stop in the old City. The building dates from 1885, and inside, tall mahogany screens divide the bar into separate sections, an unusual layout that CAMRA counts among the best-preserved pub interiors in the country.

The site's history goes back much further. In the early 1650s a coffee seller named Pasqua Rosée opened London's first coffee house here, and a plaque marks the spot. It became the Jamaica Coffee House in the 1670s and a wine house in 1869. Don't be fooled by signs implying the pub itself dates from 1652: the original building burned in the Great Fire of 1666.

On a weekday evening it fills with City workers spilling out into the alley. At weekends the whole area empties out, and the Jampot is closed.

**Our tip:** It's open Monday to Friday only. Pair it with a wander through Leadenhall Market, a couple of minutes' walk away.
