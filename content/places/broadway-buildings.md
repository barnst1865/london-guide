Come out of St James's Park station, turn around, and you are looking at the building where the Secret Intelligence Service lived for forty years. Broadway Buildings, a nine-story office block finished in 1924, housed MI6 from the mid-1920s until the move to Century House in 1964. The codebreakers of GC&CS, the people who went on to Bletchley Park, worked on another floor until 1939. During the war the brass plate by the door read "Minimax Fire Extinguisher Company," which fooled nobody who mattered.

Kim Philby, who worked here, called it "a dingy building, a warren of wooden partitions and frosted glass windows, served by an ancient lift." The chief, Stewart Menzies, is said to have reached his house at 21 Queen Anne's Gate, around the corner, through a connecting passage; that house had belonged to the first "C," Mansfield Cumming, who died before the move.

Today it is just offices, renumbered 50 Broadway, so there is nothing to go into. Across the road, 55 Broadway, London Underground's 1929 headquarters and the city's first "skyscraper," is the better building, and [St Ermin's Hotel](place:st-ermins-hotel) is a two-minute walk.

**Our tip:** Pair it with St Ermin's and the [War Rooms](place:churchill-war-rooms) for a half-mile of secret Westminster.
