One of the smallest and best pub interiors in London. The Red Lion was built in 1821 and refitted in 1871, when Walter Gibbs and Sons of Blackfriars lined every wall with etched and diamond-cut mirrors in mahogany frames, so that the one little room seems to go on forever. It is Grade II listed and on CAMRA's national inventory of historic interiors, and it is still, pleasingly, just a Fuller's pub where St James's office workers stand three deep on a Friday.

The spy connection is thinner. Frank Bossard was a Ministry of Aviation guided-weapons official who photographed secret files for the Soviet Union and was arrested in a Bloomsbury hotel room in 1965; he got 21 years. The published accounts say he was first approached by a Russian "after a few nights in a local bar," and the Red Lion is said to be where he met his handlers, though we have yet to find a source that names it. Enjoy the mirrors regardless; the espionage is a bonus.

Not to be confused with the Red Lion on Waverton Street in Mayfair, which is a different pub entirely.

**Our tip:** Go mid-afternoon on a weekday when you can actually see the mirrors. It is a ten-minute walk to [Carlton Gardens](place:carlton-gardens-berlin-tunnel) for a second Cold War stop.
