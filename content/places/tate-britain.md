Tate Britain is a big-name museum, and we include it here for one specific reason: walk along Atterbury Street, the quiet road on its western side, and look at the Portland stone. It is pockmarked from top to bottom. A high-explosive bomb on 16 September 1940 wrecked the offices, blew out every pane of glass in the building and peppered this wall; incendiaries came through the roof that December and another bomb landed in front in May 1941. The collection had already been sent to country houses and a slate mine in Wales, so the pictures survived; the wall was simply never made good, and the gallery now points it out itself.

There is a happy accident for the next year or so: with the grand Millbank entrance closed for garden works until 2027, everyone goes in through the Manton entrance on Atterbury Street, which means every visitor now files past the shrapnel whether they know it or not.

Inside is the free national collection of British art from the Tudors on, with the best Turners anywhere in the Clore Gallery, and it is far less crowded than Tate Modern downriver.

**Our tip:** Pimlico station is the nearest. Combine with the river walk down to Vauxhall Bridge for the [MI6 and MI5 buildings](place:vauxhall-cross-thames-house), ten minutes south.
