A grand Victorian mansion block turned hotel, hidden down a cul-de-sac behind St James's Park station, and for a few years the most interesting address in British intelligence. In 1938 MI6's new sabotage outfit, Section D, took rooms here, and when it was merged into the Special Operations Executive in 1940 the early SOE worked out of the hotel too, a few hundred yards from MI6's own headquarters on [Broadway](place:broadway-buildings).

In the summer of 1940 a young journalist called Kim Philby was interviewed over the hotel's tea tables by Marjorie Maxse, whom he later described as "an intensely likeable elderly lady," with his friend Guy Burgess sitting in at the second meeting. He got the job. The Soviet Union, in effect, got MI6.

The hotel knows its history and wears it lightly: a free display case of SOE memorabilia in the lobby, a bar menu that names cocktails after the Cambridge Five, and a parliamentary Division Bell that still rings for Commons votes. Some of the stories the hotel tells about itself (tunnels to Parliament, Burgess passing files in the bar) are lore rather than history, and we say so.

**Our tip:** Walk in like you belong there. The lobby display is free and the Caxton Bar is a very good place to recover from Westminster.
