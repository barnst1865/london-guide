A Maid of Honour is a small curd tart in puff pastry, and this tearoom on Kew Road is the place to try one. The Newens bakery business began in Richmond in 1850 and moved to this shop on Kew Road in 1887, according to Richmond's local history library (the shop gives an earlier date). A wartime bomb badly damaged the building, which was rebuilt afterward with the mock-Tudor front you see today.

The story that Henry VIII named the tart after Anne Boleyn's maids of honour at Richmond Palace is legend. What's documented is a recipe in a cookbook of 1665, and Richmond bakers selling the tarts commercially by 1750. Inside it's a proper English tearoom, with cakes, scones, cream teas and light lunches.

**Our tip:** It's on Kew Road between the station and Kew Green, a few minutes from the gardens' Victoria Gate. Book ahead for afternoon tea on weekends.
