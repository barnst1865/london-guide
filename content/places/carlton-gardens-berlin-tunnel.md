Carlton Gardens is a short, grand dead-end off the Mall, and in the 1950s MI6 kept offices here. In December 1953 American and British officers met in them to plan Operation Gold, known in London as Stopwatch: a 500-yard tunnel from the American sector of Berlin under the Soviet one, to tap the Red Army's telephone cables. The junior MI6 officer taking the minutes was George Blake. In January 1954 he handed a copy to his KGB controller on the top deck of a London bus. The Soviets let the tunnel be dug anyway, to protect Blake, and "discovered" it in April 1956. Blake was unmasked in 1961, sentenced to 42 years, and escaped from Wormwood Scrubs in 1966 with a rope ladder made of knitting needles. By several accounts he confessed in the very room where the tunnel was planned.

Most sources put the meetings at No. 2, which is today the offices of the Institute for Government, a charity; MI6's post-war Section Y is sometimes placed at No. 3 next door, which is now a private house. Either way this is a pavement stop. The statue outside No. 4 is de Gaulle, whose Free French ran their war from here.

**Our tip:** Come down from Piccadilly through the [Red Lion](place:red-lion-duke-of-york-street) on Duke of York Street, then drop to the Mall and the parks.
