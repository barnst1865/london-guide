The RAF Museum sits on what was Hendon aerodrome, where Claude Grahame-White was flying from 1911, and it is free, which for a collection this size is remarkable. Hangar 1 tells the RAF's first hundred years and lets you walk straight through the hull of a Short Sunderland flying boat, bunks and galley included. Hangars 3 to 5 are the main event for us: a Spitfire, a Hurricane and a Messerschmitt 109 from the Battle of Britain, the Lancaster "S for Sugar" with 137 bomb symbols painted on its nose, a Mosquito and a B-17.

For the Cold War, head to the Bomber Hall end of Hangar 5 and stand under the bomb bay of Avro Vulcan XL318. The delta-winged Vulcan carried Britain's airborne nuclear deterrent until Polaris submarines took over in 1969 and then, in 1982, flew the longest bombing raids in history to the Falklands. The Lightning interceptor nearby is the other half of the story. Hangar 6 brings things up to the present with Tornado and Typhoon.

Kids get a good aviation playground and paid extras like a sit-in Spitfire cockpit; the 4D cinema was closed for refurbishment when we last checked. Allow half a day. Colindale on the Northern line is a 15-minute walk or a short bus ride.

**Our tip:** Go on a weekday in term time and you can have the Lancaster to yourself. Pair with the [Battle of Britain Bunker](place:battle-of-britain-bunker) for a full Fighter Command day.
