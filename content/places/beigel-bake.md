Beigel Bake (yes, beigel, the old East End Jewish spelling) never closes. It opened in 1974 in a former butcher's shop at the top of Brick Lane, it's still run by the Cohen family, and at any hour there's a line of cabbies, office workers, clubbers and families shuffling past the counter.

The order is the hot salt beef beigel: thick slices of brisket carved in front of you, piled into a beigel and topped with English mustard and a gherkin. Smoked salmon and cream cheese is the gentler option, and there are cheap cakes and rugelach by the till. Don't confuse it with the yellow-fronted Beigel Shop two doors down. Locals argue over which is better; this, the white one, is ours.

It's takeaway only, so eat on the street and wander on down Brick Lane, past the curry houses and the old Truman Brewery.

**Our tip:** Order a salt beef beigel with mustard and pickle. The line moves fast. Bring some cash, just in case.
