The Old Bell is a snug, uneven little pub with a back entrance on the alley that leads to St Bride's, the Wren church whose tiered spire is said to have inspired the shape of the wedding cake. Historic England dates the building to the late 17th century, though it has been much altered since.

The story goes that Christopher Wren built it to house and refresh the masons rebuilding St Bride's after the Great Fire. It's a nice tale, and the timing fits, but nothing in the records confirms it.

Today it's a Nicholson's pub serving pies and a decent range of ales, open all week. On a walk along Fleet Street it makes a good quick stop between the Cheshire Cheese and the Black Friar, and a reason to step into St Bride's, the journalists' church, whose crypt holds Roman and medieval remains.

**Our tip:** Leave by the back entrance on St Bride's Avenue and look inside the church.
