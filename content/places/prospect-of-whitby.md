The Prospect of Whitby is the classic old Thames pub: flagstone floors, beams, a pewter-topped bar standing on old barrels, and a terrace and upstairs windows looking straight out over the river. Historic England dates the building to around 1520, with a front refaced in the 19th century. The pub calls itself the oldest riverside tavern in the country (the Mayflower, across the river, makes the same claim).

Its stories are best taken with a pinch of salt. It's said to have been known as the Devil's Tavern, a haunt of smugglers and river thieves. The noose hanging over the water recalls Execution Dock, where pirates were hanged, but the dock was actually a little way upriver and the noose is a modern prop. Pepys, Dickens, Turner and Whistler are all said to have drunk or sketched here. The name, at least, is on the record: it comes from the *Prospect*, a ship registered in Whitby that used to moor outside.

**Our tip:** Walk on to [The Grapes](place:the-grapes-limehouse), about 20 minutes east along the river through Wapping and Limehouse. Kids are welcome until early evening.
