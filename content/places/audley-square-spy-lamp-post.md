Nothing to see here, which is exactly the point. On the little square where South Audley Street kinks past the University Women's Club stands a Victorian cast-iron lamp standard with a small door in its base. The story, first set down in Roy Berkeley's 1994 walking guide *A Spy's London* and repeated ever since, is that Soviet "illegals" used the compartment as a dead-letter box from the 1950s on: leave your microfilm inside, chalk a mark on the post, and your contact would know to collect. Sources differ on which post, and the whole tale rests on that one book, so file it under "said to be" and enjoy it anyway.

What is certain is the neighborhood. [Leconfield House](place:leconfield-house), MI5's headquarters for the whole of the early Cold War, is two minutes away on Curzon Street, so if the lamp post was a drop it was a brazen one. And in the early 1960s the producers of the Bond films had their offices at No. 3, a few doors along, which is the kind of coincidence London does well.

**Our tip:** Five minutes, tops. Chain it with Leconfield House and the Litvinenko hotel for a tight Mayfair spy loop before lunch.
