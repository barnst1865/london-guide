Walk from the [Churchill War Rooms](place:churchill-war-rooms) along Horse Guards Avenue and you pass the Second World War's underground headquarters' Cold War successor without knowing it. The Ministry of Defence Main Building is the great Portland-stone slab between Whitehall and the river, designed before the First World War, started in 1938 and not finished until 1959. Two forty-ton stone figures, *Earth* and *Water*, flank the north entrance.

Beneath it lies PINDAR. Almost everything the public knows comes from a single written answer in Parliament in April 1994: a "protected crisis management facility," operational since 7 December 1992, cost £126 million. Size, depth and layout were refused as matters of "operational capability," and that is where the official record stops. Planning is understood to have started in 1979, with construction from 1984; the budget was reported to have tripled along the way.

Also down there, more cheerfully, is Henry VIII's wine cellar from Cardinal Wolsey's York Place: a 1530s brick undercroft that engineers in 1949 encased in concrete, jacked up, slid sideways and lowered into the new basement rather than demolish.

This is an active government headquarters. We describe what is on the public record and what you can see from the pavement, and nothing else.

**Our tip:** Best viewed from the Whitehall gardens on the Embankment side, then cross to the river for the [Battle of Britain Monument](place:battle-of-britain-monument).
