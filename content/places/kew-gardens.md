The Royal Botanic Gardens at Kew are a UNESCO World Heritage Site and a working scientific institution, but you don't need to care about either to love the place. Princess Augusta started a botanic garden here in 1759, and it's now a huge, beautiful landscape to wander.

The highlights:
- The Palm House (1844–48), a Grade I iron-and-glass greenhouse full of steamy rainforest.
- The Temperate House, the largest surviving Victorian glasshouse in the world.
- The Treetop Walkway, 18 meters up in the canopy.
- The Great Pagoda of 1762.
- The Hive, a walk-in sculpture inspired by beehives.

Under-12s should head for the Children's Garden, which is included with entry; teens usually like the walkway and the Hive. The gardens are far too big to see in one go, so pick a few things and leave time to sit on the grass.

**Our tip:** Book tickets online (it's cheaper than paying at the gate) and use Victoria Gate, five minutes from Kew Gardens station. Leave the dog at home: only assistance dogs are allowed, and bikes and scooters have to stay at the gate.
