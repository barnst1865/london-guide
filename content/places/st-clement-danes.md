"Oranges and lemons, say the bells of St Clement's": the bells still play the tune through the day from the steeple James Gibbs added in 1719 to Wren's church of 1682. On 10 May 1941, the last and worst night of the Blitz, incendiaries gutted the building and left only the walls and tower standing. It stood as a shell for a decade until the Royal Air Force adopted it, and in 1958 it was reconsecrated as the Central Church of the RAF in front of the Queen. A bombed church became the memorial of the people who bombed back; the symmetry is not lost on anyone.

Inside, the floor is Welsh slate inlaid with more than a thousand hand-carved squadron and unit badges, so you literally walk across the air force. Glass-fronted shrines around the walls hold the Books of Remembrance with the names of more than 150,000 RAF dead, and there are memorials to the Polish Air Force and to the Americans who flew from Britain in the Second World War; the organ was a gift from the US Air Force. Outside on the traffic island stands Gladstone, looking cross.

It is not open every day: typically Wednesday to Sunday in the middle of the day, and closed for services and events, so check the website first.

**Our tip:** Come from the Aldwych side, cross to the island, and give yourself twenty minutes inside. [Waterloo Bridge](place:markov-waterloo-bridge) and the river are three minutes south.
