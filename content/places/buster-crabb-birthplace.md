We will be honest: there is nothing to see on Greyswood Street, a terrace of ordinary houses between Streatham and Tooting, and there is no plaque. But Lionel "Buster" Crabb was born here in January 1909, and his is one of the strangest unsolved stories of the Cold War, so it earns a pin for the completists.

Crabb was a Royal Navy diver who made his name clearing Italian limpet mines from ships at Gibraltar during the war. In April 1956 the Soviet cruiser *Ordzhonikidze* brought Khrushchev and Bulganin to Portsmouth on a goodwill visit. On 19 April Crabb, by then 47 and out of condition, went into the harbor on an MI6 job to look at the ship's hull and propellers, and never came up. The government first denied everything, then Prime Minister Eden told the Commons it was "not in the public interest" to say what happened. The head of MI6 lost his job. Fourteen months later a headless, handless body in a frogman's suit was found in Chichester Harbour and buried as Crabb. Theories still multiply.

This is a residential street, so we give you the street and not the house.

**Our tip:** Only for a dedicated spy pilgrim passing through; Tooting Bec Common and the lido are the nearby reasons to be here anyway.
