The Churchill War Rooms are where the war was run; this is where the Battle of Britain was fought. Sixty feet under what was RAF Uxbridge, No. 11 Group's operations room controlled every fighter squadron defending London and the south-east through Dunkirk, the summer of 1940, Dieppe and D-Day. You go down 76 steps with a guide and come out in a room preserved as it was on the afternoon of 15 September 1940, when Churchill stood on the gallery and watched every squadron light go red, meaning nothing left in reserve. The plotters moving the counters on the big table below were mostly young WAAF women in their late teens and twenties, working through the raids by magnetic rod and telephone.

The famous line was first said here, not in Parliament: driving away after an earlier visit on 16 August, Churchill told his aide General Ismay, "Never in the field of human conflict was so much owed by so many to so few," and used it in the Commons four days later.

Hillingdon Council runs it well, with a visitor center opened in 2018 (interactive, good for younger children) and full-size replica Spitfire and Hurricane outside. Under-16s go free. The bunker itself is a guided tour of about an hour, so book a slot.

**Our tip:** A 20-minute walk from Uxbridge station through the park. Pair with the [RAF Museum](place:raf-museum-london) or [Bentley Priory](place:bentley-priory-museum), which was Fighter Command's HQ: raw radar plots were filtered there before they reached this table.
