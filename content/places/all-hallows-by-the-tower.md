All Hallows was founded around 675, three centuries before the Tower of London went up next door, and it has been collecting history ever since: Pepys watched the Great Fire from its tower, beheaded Tower prisoners were brought here before burial, William Penn was baptized here and John Quincy Adams was married here. Then in 1940 the Blitz gutted it, and the bombing did one unexpected kindness. When the rubble was cleared, an arch built of reused Roman tiles came to light in the west wall, Saxon work from the seventh century, the oldest piece of church fabric in London and completely unknown until the war exposed it. The church was rebuilt around it and rededicated in 1957.

Go down into the crypt museum for a Roman pavement, Saxon crosses and a model of Roman London, then stand at the back of the nave and look at the arch. It is free, open daily, and because it is so close to the Tower most visitors walk straight past it toward the ticket queues.

**Our tip:** The natural last stop on the [Blitz scars walk](trail:blitz-scars-walk), four minutes from [St Dunstan-in-the-East](place:st-dunstan-in-the-east), with Tower Hill station right outside.
