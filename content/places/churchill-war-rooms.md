Yes, it's on every list. Go anyway, and go for the small things. The Cabinet War Rooms ran around the clock from 27 August 1939 until 16 August 1945, when someone switched off the Map Room lights for the first time in six years and everyone went home. The room has been left as it was that day: pins, colored wool, a bank of telephones, and a Wing Commander's hoarded sugar cubes found in a drawer decades later.

Our angle is the broom cupboard. Off the main corridor is a tiny door with an engaged/vacant lock, like a toilet. Behind it was the Transatlantic Telephone Room, a scrambled line to Roosevelt in the White House, disguised so nobody would ask questions. Also look for the weather board by the Map Room: staff set it to "windy" when bombs were falling on Whitehall above. The Churchill Museum added in 2005 is excellent but long; teenagers love the interactive timeline, under-8s will flag.

The whole complex sits under the Treasury. Afterward, walk past the [Ministry of Defence](place:mod-main-building-pindar) to see where its Cold War successor went.

**Our tip:** Book a timed ticket well ahead, go at opening, and allow two and a half hours. The café is better than it needs to be.
