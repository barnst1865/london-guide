Bar Italia is the Soho we like best: neon sign, chrome Gaggia machine, a terrazzo floor laid by a member of the family, and a crowd that changes completely between breakfast and 3am. Lou and Caterina Polledri opened it in 1949, and their grandchildren still run it. The room has barely changed, and it stays open almost all night.

Look up as you go in. The blue plaque on the building records that in 1926, in the attic rooms upstairs, John Logie Baird gave the first public demonstration of television to members of the Royal Institution.

Come for the room rather than a meal. An espresso at the counter and a pastry is the whole point, whether that's mid-afternoon or after the theater.

**Our tip:** Drink your coffee standing at the bar, Italian-style; it's quicker and cheaper than a table. Late at night it's a bar crowd, so bring kids earlier in the day.
