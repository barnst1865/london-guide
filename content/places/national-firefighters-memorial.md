On the pedestrian way between the south side of St Paul's and the Millennium Bridge, three larger-than-life bronze firemen in steel helmets brace a hose, two holding the branch and an officer directing, with the cathedral dome rising behind them. The placing is deliberate: these are the men who kept St Paul's standing. The figures were modeled on a photograph of a relief crew damping down in Cannon Street on 17 April 1941, the morning after one of the heaviest raids, and one of the faces belongs to Cyril Demarne, a real Blitz fire officer who was the sculptor John Mills's father-in-law. Churchill's phrase for them, "the heroes with grimy faces," is on the base.

It was unveiled by the Queen Mother in 1991 as the Blitz memorial, carrying the names of 1,027 firefighters killed in the war, including the women of the fire service shown in relief on the plinth. In 2003 it was raised a meter and rededicated as the national memorial to all British firefighters killed on duty, with 1,192 more names.

For us it is the starting gun of the [Blitz scars walk](trail:blitz-scars-walk): everything you see for the next two hours was saved, or lost, by people like these.

**Our tip:** Stand with your back to the river so the dome is behind the hose; that is the shot. Then up Sermon Lane and across to [Christchurch Greyfriars](place:christchurch-greyfriars).
