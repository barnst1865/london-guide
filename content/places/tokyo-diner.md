This is where we go when we're in the West End and want a quick, honest meal that won't cost much, and our kids like it as much as we do. Tokyo Diner has served Japanese home cooking on the corner of Chinatown since 1992. The owner tells the story of opening it in the launderette below his Soho flat after a trip to Japan, and it still feels like a neighborhood canteen: wooden booths, free green tea, and staff who all speak Japanese.

The menu is comfort food rather than sushi theater: curry rice, donburi bowls, noodle soups and bento boxes. It's quick, so it works before a show, after a museum, or when everyone is flagging at once. It doesn't take bookings, and the house policy has always been no tipping.

**Our tip:** Don't leave a tip; they mean it. Katsu curry or a beef donburi is the classic order, and turnover is fast if there's a short wait at the door.
