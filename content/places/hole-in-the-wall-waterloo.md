Right across from Waterloo station's Victory Arch, the Hole in the Wall sits inside a brick railway arch, and trains rumble overhead every few minutes. It's a proper, unpretentious pub, with a long row of hand pumps and a rugby-themed front bar, and it's one of our favorite places to wait for a train or start a walk along the South Bank.

The name goes back to at least 1869, when a Hole in the Wall stood just around the corner; the pub had moved into this arch by the 1930s. In the early 1970s it became one of London's pioneering real-ale pubs, when a beer campaigner persuaded the landlord to stock cask beer from Young's, and it has been a stop for beer lovers ever since.

A London brewery took over the pub at the end of 2025 and plans to give it a more modern feel, so expect some changes.

**Our tip:** It's card only. Kids are welcome until early evening, and dogs are welcome too.
