The Barbican estate was built on ground the Blitz cleared, and in the middle of it, on a terrace beside the lake and the arts center, stands the one thing that was there before: a sixteenth-century church. St Giles escaped the Great Fire of 1666 by a few hundred yards, only to be hit in August 1940 and then gutted by incendiaries in the great fire raid of 29 December 1940, which destroyed the whole surrounding neighborhood of Cripplegate. The stone walls and tower stood, and the church was restored between 1960 and 1968 to its 1545 plan while the concrete towers rose around it.

John Milton is buried here, Oliver Cromwell was married here, and Shakespeare attended a nephew's baptism. Fragments of the Roman and medieval city wall run along the lake just outside. The contrast between the medieval church, the Brutalist flats and the water is one of London's great views, and almost nobody outside the City knows it.

It is a short detour north from [St Mary Aldermanbury](place:st-mary-aldermanbury-garden) on the [Blitz scars walk](trail:blitz-scars-walk), well worth it if the church is open.

**Our tip:** Approach along the Barbican highwalks from Moorgate for the full effect of the church appearing below you across the lake.
