Britain's two intelligence services have, since 1994, sat in plain sight on opposite banks of the Thames, and the best place to take them both in is the pavement of Vauxhall Bridge. Downstream on the south bank is the SIS Building at Vauxhall Cross, Terry Farrell's stepped green-and-cream ziggurat, opened by the Queen in 1994 and nicknamed Legoland and Babylon-on-Thames before it had finished drying. If it looks familiar, that is because it has played itself in every Bond film since *GoldenEye*, up to and including being blown up in *Skyfall*. In real life it took one hit: a rocket-propelled grenade fired by dissident republicans struck an eighth-floor window in September 2000 and did superficial damage.

Upstream on the north bank, by Lambeth Bridge, is Thames House, MI5's headquarters. It is older than it looks important: built in 1929–30 for the chemicals giant ICI, Grade II listed, with Charles Sargeant Jagger's St George and Britannia over the doors, and converted for the Security Service in the early 1990s. *Spooks* fans will recognize the exterior.

Both are working government buildings. This is a view, not a visit; what we know about them is what the services publish themselves.

**Our tip:** Walk north from Vauxhall station across the bridge, then along Millbank past [Tate Britain](place:tate-britain) to Lambeth Bridge for the Thames House angle.
