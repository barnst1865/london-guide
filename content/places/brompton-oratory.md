The Church of the Immaculate Heart of Mary, which nobody calls anything but the Brompton Oratory, is the most Roman building in London: a vast Baroque nave finished in 1884, with seventeenth-century marble Apostles that came from Siena Cathedral. It is worth a visit on its own terms, and it is a working church with daily Mass and a famous sung Latin liturgy on Sunday mornings, so behave accordingly.

The spy story comes, like the [Audley Square lamp post](place:audley-square-spy-lamp-post), from Roy Berkeley's 1994 book *A Spy's London*: the KGB is said to have used the space between the pillars and the wall of the front portico as a dead-letter box, with Harrods next door handy for shaking off a tail in the crowds. Later retellings move the drop just inside the door; nobody has produced a document. We tell it as the well-worn London legend it is.

Afterward, walk 200 yards along to the [V&A's shrapnel-scarred wall](place:v-and-a-blitz-wall) on Exhibition Road: two very different kinds of Cold War and wartime history, one block apart.

**Our tip:** Go in the quiet of a weekday afternoon. If you want the full Oratory experience, the 11:00 Sunday Latin Mass is extraordinary, but it is a service, not a show.
