This is the one we keep going back to. The National Army Museum reopened in 2017 after a complete rebuild and did the hard thing well: it is about soldiers rather than generals, it is honest about empire and about the home front, and it is free. Five galleries (Formation, Soldier, Global Role, Conflict in Europe and Army at Home) walk from the New Model Army to Afghanistan without ever feeling like a trudge.

The two things everyone remembers are in Conflict in Europe: the skeleton of Marengo, Napoleon's horse, captured after Waterloo and missing two hooves that became snuff boxes, and William Siborne's enormous 1830s model of the battlefield, built from questionnaires sent to surviving officers; Wellington is said to have hated it. The thing most people miss is the Victoria Cross dredged out of the Thames in the Soldier gallery. Teenagers do well with the uniforms and kit you can try, and a Challenger tank sits outside.

For small children, Play Base is a soldier-themed soft-play for under-8s with hourly sessions, booked separately and worth it. Next door is the Royal Hospital, home of the Chelsea Pensioners in their scarlet coats.

**Our tip:** Closed Mondays. Book Play Base ahead for weekends, then walk the Royal Hospital grounds and down to the river.
