RAF Northolt was a fighter station in the Battle of Britain, and the squadron that fought from it with the most success was Polish: No. 303 Squadron, pilots who had already lost their country once and flew Hurricanes with a ferocity that alarmed their British commanders. Seven Polish squadrons were based here over the course of the war. After it ended, the Polish airmen who had died for Britain could not go home to a communist Poland, and this memorial, unveiled in November 1948 at the airfield's south-east corner, became their monument instead.

It is a tall Portland stone column by Mieczysław Lubelski with a bronze eagle on top, a pond in front, and granite walls added in 1996 to carry some 1,900 names. It is listed Grade II*, one of the very few war memorials to a foreign air force to be so honored, and the junction is simply called "Polish War Memorial" on road signs and bus stops.

Northolt is still an active RAF station, used today for transport and VIP flights, so what you see is what you see from the memorial and the public road; we describe nothing more. An old draft of ours called it a Cold War nuclear bomber base, which it never was.

**Our tip:** Ten minutes' walk from South Ruislip station. Stand by the pond for a minute and you may well see something climbing out over the A40.
