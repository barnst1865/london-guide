Low beams, dark paneling and a jetty over the Thames, reached by a walk through Rotherhithe's old warehouse streets. A pub has stood here since at least the 1780s, when it was the Shippe, later the Spread Eagle and Crown. After war damage it was refitted in 1957 with a mock-Tudor interior and renamed for the Pilgrims' ship.

The Mayflower connection is real but loose. The ship's master, Christopher Jones, lived in Rotherhithe and was buried at St Mary's church nearby in 1622, and the ship is thought to have started its 1620 voyage from this stretch of river, though the exact spot isn't documented. The pub's claims to be the oldest on the Thames, and that you can see the Mayflower's mooring from the jetty, are legend.

**Our tip:** Grab a spot on the jetty if the weather's kind, and look into St Mary's on the way.
