Everyone comes to Greenwich for the Painted Hall, Thornhill's enormous Baroque ceiling where Nelson lay in state, and you should too. Our reason to put it in the spy theme is what was in the basement. From 1962 to 1996 the Royal Navy ran a 10-kilowatt training reactor called JASON under the King William Building, surrounded by 300 tons of steel and concrete, to teach officers heading for the nuclear submarine fleet. It was an official secret: when the building was listed Grade I in 1973 the listing said nothing about it, and when Greenwich declared itself a Nuclear Free Zone the council's boundary stopped, conveniently, at the College wall.

JASON was shut down in 1996 and taken apart by 1999, the first shore-based reactor in Britain to be fully decommissioned; the hall is now a university lecture theater, and there is no trace. The College's visitor center has a replica, or did when we last checked.

Grounds and chapel are free; the Painted Hall is a paid ticket (kids free with an adult) with a good audio guide. The whole site is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and the view back from the river, or down from the Observatory hill, is the Greenwich postcard.

**Our tip:** Arrive by Thames Clipper and leave by DLR. Stand in the Grand Square facing the Painted Hall: the reactor was under the range to your right.
