On the afternoon of 1 November 2006, former FSB officer Alexander Litvinenko sat down in the Pine Bar of what was then the Millennium Hotel with two Russians, Andrei Lugovoi and Dmitry Kovtun, and drank a few mouthfuls of green tea from a pot laced with polonium-210. He died at University College Hospital 22 days later. The public inquiry that reported in 2016 found that the two men had poisoned him, probably on FSB orders, and that the operation was "probably approved" by President Putin.

What makes this corner of Mayfair so eerie is how ordinary it is. The inquiry also found an earlier, botched attempt two weeks before, in a security firm's boardroom on nearby Grosvenor Street, and the radioactive trail the killers left ran through a Piccadilly sushi bar, several hotels and Arsenal's stadium. Litvinenko's home is a private house in north London, so we don't send anyone there.

The hotel reopened in 2019 as The Biltmore Mayfair. Grosvenor Square itself, for decades the center of the American presence in London, is worth a slow lap.

**Our tip:** This is a hotel with guests and staff. Look from the square, read the story, and move on to the [spy lamp post](place:audley-square-spy-lamp-post) five minutes south.
