Maison Bertaux is a sweet, slightly ramshackle slice of old Soho: a narrow shop crammed with cakes, a couple of tables downstairs and a few more up a creaky staircase. It has been baking in Soho since 1871 and calls itself London's oldest patisserie. Tradition has it that Monsieur Bertaux was a refugee from the Paris Commune, though nobody has turned up the paperwork.

Sisters Michele and Tania Wade have run it since 1988 (Michele started as a Saturday girl), and when Covid nearly finished it off in 2020, more than a thousand customers chipped in online to keep it going. It's a treat stop rather than a meal: French pastries, fruit tarts and cream cakes with a pot of tea.

**Our tip:** Point at whatever looks best in the window. The mille-feuille and the strawberry tart are the usual favorites. It's tiny, so don't bring a crowd.
