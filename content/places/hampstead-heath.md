The Heath feels like a piece of countryside dropped into north London. It's about 790 acres of rough grass, old hedgerows, woods and a chain of ponds, looked after by the City of London.

- **Parliament Hill:** climb it for the view south across the whole city. The sightline to St Paul's is protected, so no new building is allowed to block it.
- **Swimming:** below the hill, Londoners swim year-round in the men's and ladies' ponds and in the unheated 1938 Parliament Hill Lido, and in the mixed pond in summer. The ponds are for ages 8 and up, and some sessions need booking.
- **Kenwood House:** at the northern edge, run by English Heritage and free to enter. Its paintings include a Rembrandt self-portrait and Vermeer's *The Guitar Player*.

Dogs can run free over most of the Heath, though not in the playgrounds, the fenced conservation areas or the swimming ponds.

**Our tip:** Start at Hampstead Heath station, climb Parliament Hill, then walk north across the Heath to Kenwood and finish at the Spaniards Inn. Booking Kenwood online guarantees entry.
