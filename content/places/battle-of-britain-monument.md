Most war memorials ask you to stand back. This one makes you walk through it. The Battle of Britain Monument is a long granite wall on the riverside pavement opposite the Ministry of Defence, cut through at an angle, and set into it are Paul Day's two bronze high-relief friezes: the home front on one side, and on the other the famous "scramble," a crew of life-size pilots bursting out of the wall toward you. Children find it irresistible, which is rather the point. Around the outside are the names of nearly 3,000 aircrew who fought in the battle in 1940, from 15 countries, with Polish, Czech, American and Caribbean names among the British.

It was unveiled on 18 September 2005, the 65th anniversary, with surviving pilots present, and paid for by public subscription. The granite structure itself has a secret: it was a Victorian smoke vent for the steam trains of the District line running underneath.

A couple of hundred yards north at Whitehall Steps is the older RAF Memorial of 1923, a gilded eagle on a column facing the river and, beyond it, France.

**Our tip:** Combine with a riverside walk from Westminster Bridge past the [MoD building](place:mod-main-building-pindar) to [Cleopatra's Needle](place:cleopatras-needle) and [Waterloo Bridge](place:markov-waterloo-bridge): four stops, 20 minutes, two wars and a Cold War.
