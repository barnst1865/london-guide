During the Blitz, Victor Rothschild, heir to the banking family and himself an MI5 officer, sublet a flat on Bentinck Street to his Cambridge friend Guy Burgess, who moved in Anthony Blunt. Both were working for British intelligence by day and the Soviet Union by night, and the flat became the Cambridge spies' wartime salon. Goronwy Rees described it as "a French farce which has been injected with all the elements of political drama... Bedroom doors opened and shut; strange faces appeared and disappeared." Malcolm Muggeridge, who came to one gathering, left thinking Burgess was "morally afflicted in some way."

Blunt's MI5 colleague Guy Liddell was a friend of his, but whether he came to the parties is not documented, whatever the walking tours say.

It is a residential street, so we give you the street and not the door. Bentinck Street has form: Edward Gibbon wrote *The Decline and Fall of the Roman Empire* a couple of doors along, and Dickens lodged here too.

**Our tip:** Pair with [Home House](place:home-house-portman-square) on Portman Square, where Blunt confessed 23 years later, and Wigmore Hall around the corner if there is a lunchtime concert.
