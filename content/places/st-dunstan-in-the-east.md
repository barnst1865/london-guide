The most beautiful thing the Blitz left behind. St Dunstan-in-the-East was a medieval church patched up after the Great Fire and given a new tower and needle spire by Wren around 1700, carried on four flying buttresses so slender they look like they shouldn't work. The body of the church was rebuilt in Gothic style in 1817. In 1941 the bombs gutted it, and the spire stood through everything.

Rather than rebuild or clear the site, the City of London made the ruin a public garden, opened in 1971. Trees grow through the tall pointed windows, climbers have taken the walls, and a low fountain bubbles in the middle of what was the nave. It is tucked down a lane between the Tower and the Monument where almost nobody goes by accident, which is why City workers eat their lunch here and photographers come at dawn. It is open daily in daylight hours and free.

For us it is the natural end of the [Blitz scars walk](trail:blitz-scars-walk) and a lovely contrast to the heft of [HMS Belfast](place:hms-belfast) across the river.

**Our tip:** Early morning or late afternoon for the light through the windows. Then four minutes to [All Hallows-by-the-Tower](place:all-hallows-by-the-tower) for the Saxon arch the bombs uncovered.
