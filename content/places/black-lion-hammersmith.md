The Black Lion stands a few steps back from the river where Upper Mall turns into Hammersmith Terrace. Its big garden, shaded by an old chestnut tree, is the best place on this stretch to spread out with kids. The building is late 18th century and Grade II listed, though much altered inside, and a pub is recorded here by 1754.

At the back is a skittle alley, a traditional pub game that has almost disappeared from London; it can be hired for groups. The writer A. P. Herbert, who lived on Hammersmith Terrace and wrote the Thames novel *The Water Gipsies*, drank here, and his portrait hangs by what's said to be his corner table. The short version of our walk ends here: Stamford Brook station is about ten minutes inland.

**Our tip:** On a warm weekend the garden is a better bet for a group than the riverside pubs.
