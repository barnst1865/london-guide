The French House is one small, crowded bar on Dean Street, and it has been Soho's front room for over a century. There's been a pub here since at least the 1820s. Victor Berlemont took it over in 1914, and his son Gaston ran it until 1989. During the Second World War, Free French officers in exile gathered here, which is how the York Minster, as it was then called, became known as "the French pub." The name became official in the 1980s. The story goes that de Gaulle wrote his famous 1940 appeal to the French nation at the bar; there's no evidence he did, but you'll hear it anyway.

The house rules are part of the charm. Beer comes only in half pints, and there's no music, no TV and no phones. Order a glass of wine or a pastis instead and talk to whoever is squeezed in next to you. The 1937 interior is on CAMRA's list of nationally important pub interiors.

**Our tip:** Don't ask for a pint (the one exception is April 1). The bar doesn't take bookings, but the small dining room upstairs does.
