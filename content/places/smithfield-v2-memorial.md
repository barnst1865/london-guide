Just after eleven on the morning of Thursday 8 March 1945 the north-east corner of Smithfield Market was busier than usual: word had gone round that there were rabbits for sale, a rare thing in the sixth year of rationing, and a long queue had formed. A V-2 came through the roof of the fish and vegetable market hall and exploded in the basement, bringing the building down on the people inside. About 110 died. After the Woolworths in New Cross the previous November, it was the deadliest rocket strike on London.

The 1892 hall was never rebuilt, and for decades nothing marked the spot. When a new office block went up on the corner in the early 2020s the developers set a memorial into the pavement at its entrance: a highly polished circular disc, 1.65 meters across, which is the exact diameter of a V-2, inscribed to those affected by the attack. You will walk over it without noticing unless you know to look down.

Smithfield is changing fast: the London Museum opens in the old General Market in late 2026, and the meat market itself is due to stop trading around 2028, ending more than 800 years of livestock and meat on this spot.

**Our tip:** Farringdon station is two minutes away. Come early on a weekday while the meat market is still working, then walk down to [Christchurch Greyfriars](place:christchurch-greyfriars) and the [Blitz scars walk](trail:blitz-scars-walk).
