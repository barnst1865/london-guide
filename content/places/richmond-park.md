Richmond Park is the closest you'll get to wilderness inside London. It's about 2,360 acres of grassland, bracken and ancient oaks, enclosed by Charles I in 1637 as a hunting park. Around 600 red and fallow deer still roam it, and it's a National Nature Reserve.

From King Henry's Mound, in the gardens of Pembroke Lodge, a gap cut through the trees frames St Paul's Cathedral, 12 miles away; it's one of London's protected views. The story that Henry VIII stood here waiting for a signal that Anne Boleyn had been executed is legend. Pembroke Lodge itself is now a tearoom. The Isabella Plantation, a woodland garden of ponds and azaleas, is at its best in late April and May.

The deer are wild: stay at least 50 meters away and never feed them. Be especially careful during the autumn rut (September to November) and the spring birthing season (May to July), when dogs must be kept on leads throughout the park.

**Our tip:** Richmond station is an uphill walk of about 30 minutes from the park's Richmond Gate; take a bus if you have small kids. Dogs aren't allowed in Pembroke Lodge's gardens, so the view from King Henry's Mound is dog-free.
