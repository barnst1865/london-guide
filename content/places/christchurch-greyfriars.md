On the night of 29 December 1940, the great fire raid on the City, eight of Wren's churches burned. Christ Church Newgate Street, on the site of the medieval Franciscan friary where four queens were buried, was one of them; a firefighter ran into the blazing nave to save the wooden font cover. The tower and the north wall survived, and in 1949 the decision was taken not to rebuild.

What you get instead is one of the City's stranger pleasures. The footprint of the nave was planted in 1989 as a rose garden, with box-edged beds following the lines of the pews and ten tall wooden towers standing exactly where Wren's stone columns stood, so you read the lost church in roses and timber. The Wren tower at the west end, Grade I listed, was converted into a vertical private house in the 2000s, which means someone eats breakfast in a steeple.

A warning for 2026 and early 2027: the garden is closed while the City turns the road next to it into a new pedestrian square, Greyfriars Square, due to finish in spring 2027. The tower and the ruined walls are still perfectly visible from the Newgate Street pavement, so it stays on the [Blitz scars walk](trail:blitz-scars-walk) as a look-over-the-fence stop until it reopens.

**Our tip:** Exit St Paul's station on the Newgate Street side and it is in front of you; the [Firefighters Memorial](place:national-firefighters-memorial) is five minutes back toward the cathedral.
