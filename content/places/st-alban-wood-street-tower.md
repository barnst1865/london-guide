You come up Wood Street between the glass office blocks and there it is in the middle of the road: a 92-foot pinnacled Gothic tower with traffic going round it like a roundabout. Wren rebuilt St Alban's after the Great Fire in the 1680s and, unusually for him, did it in Gothic, supposedly to echo the medieval church it replaced. The Blitz burned the church out in 1940 and the body was demolished after the war (sources argue over whether in 1955 or 1965) when Wood Street was widened, leaving only the tower. Historic England's listing text puts it with wonderful dryness: "Church destroyed during and after World War II."

In the 1980s the tower was converted into a private house, so this is strictly a look-from-the-pavement stop, and one of the oddest sights in the City once you notice it. Most people never do.

It sits two minutes from [St Mary Aldermanbury](place:st-mary-aldermanbury-garden), another Wren church that went in the same raids and ended up, remarkably, in Missouri, and the two together make the middle of our [Blitz scars walk](trail:blitz-scars-walk).

**Our tip:** Best photographed from the south end of Wood Street looking north, with the traffic blurring past.
