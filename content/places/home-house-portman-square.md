Anthony Blunt, Surveyor of the Queen's Pictures, Director of the Courtauld Institute of Art and knight of the realm, was also a Soviet agent recruited at Cambridge in the 1930s. For 27 years he ran the Courtauld from this Robert Adam house of the 1770s, and lived in a flat on the top floor. On 23 April 1964 an MI5 officer called Arthur Martin came to that flat with an offer of immunity. Blunt asked for "five minutes while I wrestle with my conscience," stood at the window looking out over Portman Square, and confessed. The deal kept it secret for fifteen years, until Margaret Thatcher named him in the Commons in 1979.

The Courtauld moved to Somerset House in 1989, and Home House has been a private members' club since 1998, so you can't go in without a member. From the square you can admire what is arguably the best surviving Adam interior in London from the outside, which is a slightly frustrating sentence.

Ten minutes' walk east is [Bentinck Street](place:bentinck-street-flat), where Blunt and Guy Burgess shared a wartime flat, if you want the earlier chapter.

**Our tip:** The square's garden is private too; the best angle is from the pavement on the north side, looking at the plain brick front that gives nothing away.
