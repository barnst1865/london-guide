Rules opened as an oyster bar in 1798 and claims to be London's oldest restaurant (Wiltons, which started as an oyster stall in 1742, disputes the title). Either way, it's about as old-school as London dining gets: red plush, gilt, and walls covered in paintings, cartoons and prints. The current building dates from 1873.

It specializes in traditional British food, above all game in season, along with oysters, pies and puddings. Graham Greene, Evelyn Waugh and Dorothy L. Sayers all wrote it into their novels, and one of the private rooms is named for Greene. The story goes that the future Edward VII courted the actress Lillie Langtry here and slipped in through a private side door; even Historic England's listing mentions the romance, but the door itself is legend. In 1971, when the restaurant was threatened with demolition, John Betjeman wrote in protest.

It's expensive, so save it for a special meal.

**Our tip:** Book ahead, and go in game season (late summer through winter) for grouse, partridge or venison.
