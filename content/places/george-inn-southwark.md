Borough High Street was once lined with coaching inns, where travelers, coaches and goods from Kent and the south coast stopped before crossing London Bridge into the City. The George is the only one left, and the last galleried inn in London: a long range with open wooden galleries running along the upper floors, overlooking a cobbled yard. It was rebuilt after a fire swept through Southwark in 1676. In the late 1800s a railway company pulled down most of the rest of the courtyard, and the National Trust has owned what survives since 1937.

Inside it's a warren of small, dark, paneled rooms. Look for the "Parliament clock," a reminder of a short-lived 1797 tax on clocks and watches that drove people to rely on clocks in public places. Dickens mentions the George in *Little Dorrit*. You'll also hear that Shakespeare drank here, or that his plays were performed in the yard in his lifetime. That's legend, though theater companies do still perform in the yard.

**Our tip:** The yard tables are walk-in only, so come early on a warm evening. Pair it with Borough Market and Southwark Cathedral, a few minutes away.
