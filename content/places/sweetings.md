Sweetings is a time capsule of the old City lunch. It has been serving fish on this corner since 1889 (the business started as a fishmonger in 1830), and it still feels like an old City dining room: you often eat at the counter next to strangers, and there are no bookings at all. It opens for lunch on weekdays only, which tells you who it was built for.

The menu is plain British fish cookery done well: oysters, potted shrimps, smoked haddock with a poached egg, fried or grilled sole and plaice, fish pie, then a steamed pudding. Order a Black Velvet (Guinness topped up with champagne), which arrives in a pewter or silver tankard.

It isn't cheap, and it's not a place for young kids. But for an unhurried grown-up lunch in the City, there's nothing quite like it.

**Our tip:** Arrive as it opens at lunchtime to get a seat without waiting. Weekdays only.
