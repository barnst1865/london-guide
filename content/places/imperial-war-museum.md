The building was Bedlam, the Bethlem Royal Hospital for the insane, until 1930; two 15-inch naval guns from HMS *Ramillies* and HMS *Resolution* guard the lawn. Inside, the atrium hangs a Battle of Britain Spitfire that flew 57 sorties with 609 Squadron, a Harrier jump jet and a V-2 rocket over a T-34 tank, and most people never notice the Reuters Land Rover hit by rocket fire in Gaza or the bar-front from the Dambusters' officers' mess.

The Second World War Galleries, opened in 2021, are the best telling of that war in Britain: global in scope, full of ordinary people's belongings, and clever about the home front and the Blitz. Allow two hours for them alone. The Holocaust Galleries on the top floor are extraordinary and the museum recommends them for 14 and over; take that seriously. The old "Secret War" espionage gallery closed a few years ago and new Cold War galleries are planned but not yet open.

For kids, the atrium and the First World War galleries work from about seven; the Second World War galleries from about ten. It is free and rarely needs booking.

**Our tip:** Go on a weekday morning, start on the top floors and work down, and leave the atrium for last when it has emptied out. [HMS Belfast](place:hms-belfast) is a 25-minute walk along the river for a full IWM day.
