Gordon's is one of our favorite bars in the world. Go down the steps from Villiers Street into low, candlelit brick vaults with the tables packed close together. It's at its best late in the evening, when the cellar is loud and glowing. There's no beer on tap: this is a wine bar, opened in 1890 by Angus Gordon, and it calls itself London's oldest. (The Gordon family that has run it since the 1970s is no relation; the name was a happy coincidence.) Try the port, sherry or madeira poured from the cask. It's an experience. The food is simple: cheese, charcuterie and pies.

Rudyard Kipling lived in the building above from 1889 to 1891 and wrote his first novel there; look for the blue plaque. Outside there's a big terrace beside the York Watergate, built in the 1620s as the river entrance to a grand mansion, and left high and dry when the Victorians built the Embankment.

**Our tip:** It doesn't take bookings, so come early or late to get a table in the vaults, or grab a terrace table on a warm evening. Card only.
