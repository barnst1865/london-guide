Off the King's Road, Carlyle Square is one of those Chelsea garden squares that look like a stage set, and for a few years after the war it was home to the most successful Soviet agent in British history. Kim Philby and his wife Aileen lived in a house here in the mid-1940s; their wedding reception was held in it in September 1946, by which time Philby was head of MI6's section for countering Soviet espionage, a job that must have been very convenient. He was posted to Istanbul in 1947 and later to Washington, and the long unraveling began with the flight of Burgess and Maclean in 1951.

The famous scene most people associate with Philby in London, the 1955 press conference in which he denied ever having been a communist with a straight face and a stammer, happened a mile away in his mother's flat in Drayton Gardens, not here. He defected to Moscow from Beirut in 1963.

The houses are private homes and the garden is residents-only, so this is a walk-around-the-square stop. Fittingly, the chairman of the wartime Joint Intelligence Committee lived on the same square.

**Our tip:** Combine with the [National Army Museum](place:national-army-museum), ten minutes' walk away on Royal Hospital Road.
