Walk past this corner of Curzon Street and South Audley Street and you would never guess. Leconfield House, a plain stone-faced block built just before the war on part of the site of the demolished Chesterfield House, was the headquarters of the Security Service from 1945 to 1976. The hunt for the Cambridge spies, the Profumo affair, the early Cold War's double agents and defectors: all of it was run from offices behind these windows, before MI5 moved on to Gower Street and then, in 1994, to [Thames House](place:vauxhall-cross-thames-house).

Don't confuse it with Curzon Street House a little further east, a different building that MI5 used for registry and technical work from 1978. Leconfield House today is ordinary private offices, so this is strictly a look-from-the-pavement stop. There is no plaque and there has never been one.

The reason to come is the geometry. The [Audley Square lamp post](place:audley-square-spy-lamp-post), said to be a KGB dead-letter box, is barely two hundred yards away, and the hotel where [Litvinenko](place:biltmore-mayfair-litvinenko) was poisoned is a five-minute walk north. Mayfair was, and is, a very small world.

**Our tip:** Come as part of the Mayfair loop; on its own it is a building. Shepherd Market, just behind, is good for lunch.
