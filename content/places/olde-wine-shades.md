Down a quiet lane off Cannon Street, the Olde Wine Shades is narrow, dark and wood-paneled, with a cellar bar below. Historic England dates the building to the late 17th century, and the bar claims a founding date of 1663, which would make it older than the Great Fire.

That's where the stories start. The bar is often said to be one of the very few City buildings to survive the fire of 1666, but Martin Lane lay right in the fire's path, a few hundred yards from Pudding Lane, and a late-17th-century building fits a post-fire rebuild just as well, so treat that one as legend. Dickens is said to have drunk here, and there are tales of a bricked-up smugglers' tunnel to the river; neither is proven.

It's a wine bar first (run by the wine merchants Davy's), but it pours beer too and serves sandwiches and small plates.

**Our tip:** It's open Monday to Friday only. Head down to the cellar bar, then walk two minutes to the Monument itself.
