This is the easiest way to see a quieter London. Start at Little Venice, where the Regent's Canal meets the Paddington arm in a pool lined with houseboats. The name is often credited to Robert Browning, who lived nearby, though it probably only caught on after the Second World War. The canal opened from here to Camden in 1816, and you follow its towpath east for about two miles.

Early on you have to leave the water where the canal vanishes into the Maida Hill tunnel: cross Edgware Road, then drop back down to the towpath by a steep flight of steps (awkward with a stroller). After that come leafy cuttings and grand villas backing onto the water, then a stretch through London Zoo, where colobus monkeys live in the old aviary beside the towpath. Look up at Macclesfield Bridge, known as "Blow Up Bridge" since a barge carrying gunpowder exploded beneath it in 1874. The walk ends in the crowds of Camden Lock and its market.

**Our tip:** If little legs give out, canal boats run between Little Venice and Camden for much of the year (check the operators' websites). They don't stop at the zoo.
