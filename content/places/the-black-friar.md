The Black Friar may be the most joyful pub interior in London. It was built in the 1870s on the site of a medieval Dominican friary (the friars' black robes gave Blackfriars its name), and in the early 1900s the architect H. Fuller Clark and the sculptor Henry Poole turned it into an Arts and Crafts showpiece. Friars are everywhere, carved, cast and set in marble, copper, bronze and mosaic, alongside carved mottos and gleaming woodwork. In the vaulted back room, known as the Grotto, the column capitals are carved with nursery rhymes and Aesop's fables.

In the 1960s the pub was threatened with demolition, and the poet John Betjeman is credited with leading the campaign that saved it. It's now Grade II* listed, and its interior is among CAMRA's highest-rated in the country. Unlike most City pubs, it's open at weekends too.

**Our tip:** Look up at the outside before you go in, then walk through to the Grotto at the back.
