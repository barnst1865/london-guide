After a walk across Hampstead Heath, the Spaniards is where we end up: a rambling inn with low ceilings, fireplaces and a big garden that works for kids and dogs. Historic England dates the building to the 17th century; the pub itself claims 1585. Across the road stands the old tollhouse, and between the two buildings the road still squeezes down to a single lane. This was the tollgate into the Bishop of London's estate.

Its literary fame is real. In *The Pickwick Papers*, Mrs. Bardell's outing to the Spaniards ends with her arrest, and in *Dracula*, Dr. Seward catches a cab "near the Spaniards." Much of the rest is folklore. Dick Turpin didn't grow up here (that was Hempstead, in Essex), and Keats wrote "Ode to a Nightingale" in his own garden down the hill, not this one. The story goes that during the Gordon Riots of 1780, the landlord kept a mob busy with free drinks until soldiers arrived, saving nearby Kenwood House from the rioters.

**Our tip:** Walk up across the Heath from Hampstead and combine it with Kenwood House, a short walk away. It gets busy on weekends, so book for Sunday lunch.
