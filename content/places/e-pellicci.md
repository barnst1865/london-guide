If you only visit one London caff (a "caff" is a cheap, all-day café, not a coffee shop), make it this one. The Pellicci family have run it since 1900; the E stands for Elide, wife of the founder. Behind the custard-yellow Vitrolite shopfront is a 1946 interior of Art Deco marquetry panels by the craftsman Achille Capocci, so rare and intact that Historic England listed it in 2005.

The food is proper East End café cooking with an Italian accent: full English breakfasts, bacon sandwiches, steak pie, lasagne and the house penne, then bread pudding with custard. It's tiny, so you'll probably share a table, and the family will chat with you whether you planned on it or not. The Kray twins are said to have been regulars.

**Our tip:** Go on a weekday morning to avoid the Saturday queue. It's daytime only and closed on Sundays, and it's about a 15-minute walk from Brick Lane.
