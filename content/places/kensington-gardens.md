Kensington Gardens runs straight on from Hyde Park but feels calmer and more formal: about 265 acres of avenues, flower walks and water, once the private grounds of Kensington Palace.

The one thing to find is George Frampton's bronze Peter Pan, beside the Long Water. J. M. Barrie had it put up overnight so that it appeared on May Day 1912 as a surprise for the children who played here. Barrie's Peter Pan stories began in these gardens. Nearby:

- The Italian Gardens (around 1860), with fountains at the head of the Long Water.
- The Round Pond, in front of the palace.
- The Albert Memorial, on the south side.
- The Diana Memorial Playground, in the northwest corner, for younger kids.

Dogs are welcome but must be on a lead around the Round Pond, the Long Water, the Italian Gardens and the flower walks.

**Our tip:** From Lancaster Gate station, start at the Italian Gardens, walk down the west side of the Long Water to Peter Pan, then cut across to the Round Pond and the playground.
