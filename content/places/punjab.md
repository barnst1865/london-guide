Punjab is the curry house we keep coming back to, and the one we send people to when they want Indian food in the middle of town without the theatrics. Gurbachan Singh Maan opened it in Aldgate in 1946 (some accounts say 1947), and in 1951 the family moved it to Neal Street, where it has been ever since. His descendants still run it, and the restaurant calls itself the oldest North Indian restaurant in the country. We can't prove that, but its age certainly makes it one of the oldest.

The dining rooms are traditional and unhurried, and the menu sticks to Punjabi home cooking: tandoori dishes, slow-cooked lamb, black dal and breads straight from the oven. It works for a family dinner, a group, or a quick bite before a show in Covent Garden.

**Our tip:** It's mostly walk-in, so arrive before 7pm or after 9pm. Get a bullet naan (green chilli and garlic) and a black dal for the table.
