This one is for our American visitors especially. St Mary Aldermanbury was rebuilt by Wren in the 1670s and gutted with seven other City churches in the fire raid of 29 December 1940. For twenty years the shell stood empty. Then Westminster College in Fulton, Missouri, where Churchill had given his "Iron Curtain" speech in 1946, asked if it could have it. In 1965 some 7,000 stones were numbered, taken down, shipped by sea to Virginia and by rail to Missouri, and put back together; Harry Truman turned the first sod in 1966 and the church was re-hallowed in 1969 as the Churchill memorial. It is still there, now America's National Churchill Museum.

What London kept is the footprint: a sunken lawn where the nave was, medieval footings in the grass, a knot garden, and a stone marking the altar. The 1896 monument with a bust of Shakespeare honors two parishioners buried here, John Heminges and Henry Condell, the actors who put together the First Folio in 1623; without them half the plays would be lost.

It is free, always open, and tucked behind Guildhall where almost nobody finds it.

**Our tip:** Sit on the bench, look at the empty lawn, and think about the same walls standing in Missouri. Then two minutes to the [St Alban tower](place:st-alban-wood-street-tower).
