On the evening of 3 March 1943 the sirens went and the people of Bethnal Green did what they had done a hundred times: they filed down the stairs of the unfinished tube station to shelter. Then a new anti-aircraft rocket battery in Victoria Park opened up with a noise nobody had heard before. The crowd surged, a woman carrying a baby fell on the dim, wet steps, and in seconds the stairwell was packed solid. 173 people died standing up, 62 of them children. Not a single bomb fell on Bethnal Green that night.

The government suppressed the news for 36 hours and sat on the inquiry report for nearly two years, worried about morale and about blame. Survivors and families campaigned for decades, and the memorial they finally raised, completed in December 2017, is one of the most affecting in London: an inverted teak staircase of exactly the original's dimensions, cantilevered over the spot, with 173 small conical holes letting light through and every name and age carved along the outside. Stand underneath and look up.

It is right at the station's south-east entrance, the one where it happened, so you walk past it on the way out.

**Our tip:** Come out of Bethnal Green station by the Museum Gardens exit and it is in front of you. Ten quiet minutes. The names and ages are hard going for children; we would say ten and up.
