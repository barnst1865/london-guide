Scott's started as John Scott's oyster rooms off the Haymarket in the 1850s, spent most of a century on Coventry Street, and moved to Mount Street in 1967. It is now the flagship of a big restaurant group, with a mosaic ceiling, a crustacea bar in the middle of the room, and prices to match the postcode. We put it here for the stories as much as the fish.

The Fleming connection is real but belongs to the old Coventry Street rooms: as a wartime naval intelligence officer he reportedly tried to loosen the tongues of captured U-boat officers over dinner there, and he had a regular first-floor corner table. The restaurant itself only says he "reportedly" came up with the shaken-not-stirred martini at Scott's, which is our cue to call it apocryphal. He died in 1964, three years before the move, so he never ate on Mount Street.

The Mount Street windows have their own history. On 12 November 1975 an IRA bomb thrown into the restaurant killed a diner, John Batey, and injured fifteen. Three weeks later the same unit fired on the restaurant from a passing car, was chased across Marylebone, and took a couple hostage in their flat: the six-day Balcombe Street siege, which ended with the gang's surrender.

**Our tip:** Skip a table and take two stools at the oyster bar; it is the atmosphere at a fraction of the bill. Book anyway.
