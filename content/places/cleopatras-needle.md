Everyone knows the obelisk: carved in Egypt around 1450 BC, given to Britain in 1819, nearly lost at sea on the way over and finally put up on the Embankment in 1878, flanked by two Victorian bronze sphinxes. Almost nobody looks at the plinth. A few minutes before midnight on 4 September 1917, in one of the first night raids on London by German Gotha bombers, a bomb landed in the roadway here as a tram went past. The driver, Alfred Buckle, and two passengers were killed; the fragments gouged the granite base, the sphinxes' pedestals and the flank of the right-hand sphinx, and they have never been repaired. A small bronze plaque explains why. (The plaque calls it the first aeroplane raid on London, which it wasn't; it was the first night one.)

So this is the First World War's mark on the city, a generation before the Blitz, and a reminder that London was being bombed from the air before most of the people who fought the second war were born.

It makes a two-minute stop on a riverside walk between the [Battle of Britain Monument](place:battle-of-britain-monument) and [Waterloo Bridge](place:markov-waterloo-bridge).

**Our tip:** The scarred sphinx is the one on the right as you face the river; run a hand over the pedestal and feel the gouges.
