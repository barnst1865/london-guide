At about 6:40 on the evening of 8 September 1944 a rocket launched five minutes earlier from a wood near The Hague came down in the middle of Staveley Road, a 1930s residential street in Chiswick. There was no warning and no engine noise: the V-2 arrived faster than sound, so people heard the explosion first and the double sonic boom after it. Three were killed, Ada Harrison, 68, who kept a sweet shop, Rosemary Clarke, aged three, asleep in her cot, and Sapper Bernard Browning, 28, home on leave; 22 were injured and eleven houses flattened. The government told the public it was a gas main and kept that line for two months, though the sudden arrival of senior officials gave the game away locally.

It was the first ballistic missile ever to strike a city. The black granite memorial on the verge beside the electricity substation was put up in 2004 by the Battlefields Trust and the local history society, with a twin stone unveiled the same evening at the launch site in Wassenaar. An information board names the dead.

This is a residential street. Please be quiet, take your photographs of the stone and not the houses, and move on.

**Our tip:** Chiswick rail station is five minutes' walk; Chiswick House and its gardens are five minutes the other way, which makes a good afternoon of it.
