Squeezed between modern buildings on Aldgate High Street, the Hoop and Grapes is a rare survivor: a timber-framed building that Historic England dates to the late 17th century, of a type once common in London and now almost gone. Aldgate lay outside the area burned in the Great Fire of 1666.

The pub tells bigger stories than that: a "1593" date on the building, 13th-century cellars, a Cromwell-era listening tube, and the fire stopping just 50 yards away. Treat them all as legend; the fire's edge was well to the west. It traded as a wine merchant for most of its life and only became a pub in 1920.

**Our tip:** It's open every day, so it's a good weekend finish when the City's other old pubs are shut. There are steps at the entrance and the restrooms are downstairs.
