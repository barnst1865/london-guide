This is the London end of the Salisbury poisonings, and it is a sobering stop rather than a fun one. On Friday 2 March 2018 two men traveling as "Alexander Petrov" and "Ruslan Boshirov" flew into Gatwick from Moscow and checked into a small hotel on Bow Road. On Saturday they took the train from Waterloo to Salisbury to look around, and came back. On Sunday they went again, smeared a military nerve agent called Novichok on the front-door handle of former Russian intelligence officer Sergei Skripal's house, returned to London, and were on the 22:30 flight out of Heathrow that night.

The Skripals survived. Four months later Dawn Sturgess, a 44-year-old mother of three who had nothing to do with any of it, died after her partner gave her a discarded perfume bottle he had picked up in Salisbury; it held enough Novichok to kill thousands. When police finally swabbed the Bow Road room in May 2018 the agent was still there, at levels judged harmless. The public inquiry into Dawn Sturgess's death reported in December 2025 that the operation was authorized at the highest level of the Russian state.

The hotel is still trading, with guests and staff who had no part in this. Read the story on the pavement and leave them be.
