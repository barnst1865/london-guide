Just after eleven on Sunday 18 June 1944, five days into the flying-bomb campaign, the Guards' Chapel was full for a service of thanksgiving for the Normandy landings when a V-1 came through the roof. 121 people were killed and 141 seriously injured: soldiers, their families, visiting officers, and the Coldstream band. It was the deadliest single V-1 strike of the war. The apse at the east end stood, its gold mosaic of Christ intact, and when rescuers got in they found the silver altar cross and six candles still standing, the candles still burning. The chapel's own website still tells that story.

The present chapel was built around the surviving apse in 1963, a clean modern box that makes the old mosaic glow. It is open to the public on weekdays and free, and the Sunday 11:00 service with the Guards' band and a professional choir is the best free concert in Westminster if you don't mind it being a service. Next door, the small Guards Museum tells the regiments' story from the Civil War on, with a toy-soldier shop that is the hook for children under ten.

Wellington Barracks is a working army barracks: the chapel and museum have their own public entrances on Birdcage Walk, and that is as far as we go.

**Our tip:** Catch the Changing of the Guard forming up on the barracks square around 10:30, then the chapel. The [War Rooms](place:churchill-war-rooms) are ten minutes across the park.
