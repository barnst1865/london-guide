HMS *Belfast* was launched in March 1938, escorted Arctic convoys to Russia, helped sink the *Scharnhorst* off North Cape on Boxing Day 1943, and at 5:27 on the morning of 6 June 1944 fired some of the first shells of D-Day at the Normandy beaches. She fought again in Korea, was paid off in 1963 and has been moored opposite the Tower of London as a museum since 1971. It is the only big-gun warship of the war you can climb all over in Britain.

And climb you will. Nine decks, steep ladders, from the open bridge down past the mess decks, sick bay, galley and chapel to the shell rooms and boiler rooms far below the waterline, which is where the point of the ship sinks in. The Operations Room recreates the North Cape battle. Our favorite fact is official: the forward six-inch guns are aimed, deliberately, at London Gateway motorway services on the M1, twelve miles away, because in 1971 someone needed a well-known landmark inside their range.

Brilliant from about six if the ladders are not a problem; under-fives are free but hard work, and the engine room is too tight for small children. Allow three hours.

**Our tip:** Book online for the cheaper ticket, go at opening, and start at the bottom while your legs are fresh. The [ruined church garden of St Dunstan-in-the-East](place:st-dunstan-in-the-east) is ten minutes across the bridge.
