# Research criteria

Reusable criteria sets for finding places. Each research session names the set it used, records the thresholds it applied, and writes its results to its own file in `planning/research/`. Add new sets below as "Criteria set 2", "Criteria set 3", and so on. Don't edit an old set's wording once results depend on it; add a revised set instead.

Meeting a set is a reason to look harder, not an automatic include (see the guide's editorial rules: well established, good value, quirky or unexpectedly good; skip chains, trendy-new and overpriced places).

---

## Criteria set 1: "The long-standing local"

**Theme:** `eat` · **Type:** `restaurant` · **From:** parents (a rule of thumb used for years)

An ethnic restaurant (Indian, Chinese, Italian, Turkish, Vietnamese, Lebanese, etc.) that:

1. **Has been at the same address for 25+ years** (opened 2001 or earlier, no moves).
2. **Has decent reviews:** roughly 4.0+ on Google with a meaningful number of reviews, or equivalent. Record the rating and count.
3. **Shows evidence that people from that cuisine's own community eat there:** reviews or press saying things like "full of Turkish families", reviews written in that language, recommendations from diaspora media or community sites, a menu or signs in the language.
4. **Is good value:** £ or ££ (`price: 1–2`), not a destination splurge.
5. **Is still open today:** confirmed by recent reviews or an active website or social account.

### Where to look

Go beyond central London. The best examples are often in neighbourhoods such as Southall, Tooting, Green Lanes, Kingsland Road, Edgware Road, Brixton, Wembley and Golders Green.

### How to verify

- **Longevity and same address:** look for the restaurant's own "since 19xx" claim, Companies House records, dated press articles, old guidebook or review mentions at the same address, and archived web pages. Record what proves the date. "Family-run for 30 years" is not proof of the same location.
- **Community patronage:** quote or paraphrase the specific evidence, with links. Don't infer it from the cuisine alone.
- **Ratings:** Google Maps pages can't be fetched directly, so use ratings quoted elsewhere (aggregators, press, the venue's own site), and say where the number came from and when.
- **Open status:** a dated recent review, an active website or a social account posting this year.

### Output format

`planning/research/<topic>.md` with a table:

name | cuisine | area | address | year opened (evidence) | same address? | rating (count) | community evidence | price | still open? | sources | criteria met (all, or which are missing)

Sorted into **Meets all criteria**, **Near misses** (one criterion uncertain) and **Rejected** (with a one-line reason).
